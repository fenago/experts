# GES Top 10 Acquisition Targets

*Prepared by: Corporate Development (corp-dev-lead), using the market-intelligence-analyst sourcing method · For: Dr. Lee, Derek Linde (CEO), the CFO and operating-company presidents · Research date: **November 19, 2026** · Stage for every name: **Sourced** (no contact made)*

---

## Executive summary

We screened 36 real companies across GES's seven priority categories and recommend that GES look first at **Map Your Show** (exhibitor floor-plan and sales software, for Visit), **Hall-Erickson** (a family-owned firm that runs and sells exhibit space for association trade shows, for CSG Creative) and **Marketing General Inc. (MGI)** (an Alexandria, VA membership-marketing agency owned by printing giant Taylor Corporation, for CSG Creative). All three fit the pattern of GES's first two acquisitions. They add a capability GES lacks, they earn steadier, more recurring revenue than show contracting, they should carry higher profit margins than GES's core business (about 5–7% EBITDA, meaning earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization), and each shows a sign the owner may be ready to sell. A private-equity owner has held Map Your Show for nearly five years. Hall-Erickson's president has run it since 1982. MGI could be "carved out" (bought from a corporate parent), the same structure GES used for CSG Creative. The rest of the top 10 cover the other gaps: a UK experiential agency (DRPG), event registration software (eShow), a West Coast exhibit builder (Pinnacle), a national show-electrical contractor (Edlen), a Saudi/UAE show contractor (ArabExpo), a rental and modular exhibit maker (Classic Exhibits) and an audio-visual (AV) rental company (Meeting Tomorrow). **Main caveat:** these companies are private, so every size figure below is our estimate, and ownership for several (ArabExpo, Classic Exhibits, Pinnacle's current sponsor, Meeting Tomorrow) could not be fully confirmed from public sources. The estimates are a starting point for confidential conversations, not facts.

**Key terms:** *Add-on* = a smaller company bought by a PE-owned platform such as GES. *Fold-in* = the target is merged into an existing GES company and loses its brand (2Heads → Spiro). *Standalone* = the target keeps its brand as its own operating company (CSG Creative). *Carve-out* = buying a division from a larger corporate parent. *GSC* = general service contractor. *HSR* = the U.S. Hart-Scott-Rodino pre-merger antitrust filing.

## 1. Method

- **Sources:** Public web only (company sites, trade press such as Exhibitor Online, Exhibit City News, TSNN, Event Marketer, Conference News, micebook, BizBash; PR Newswire; deal databases such as Mergr, CB Insights and PrivSource). No one was contacted. Several sites could not be reached directly from this research environment, so some facts rely on search-engine summaries of those pages. These are marked "[per search summary]".
- **Verification test for every candidate:** (1) the company exists and is operating in 2026; (2) no announced acquisition by Freeman, Fern/MSouth, Encore, The Expo Group, Impact XM, Cvent, Maritz or another consolidator; (3) plausibly add-on sized (about $1M–$20M EBITDA); (4) ownership type identified, or flagged as unverified.
- **Scoring model** (from `market-intelligence-analyst`). Each factor is scored 1–5 and the weighted total is out of 5.0:

| Factor | Weight |
|---|---|
| Strategic fit with a GES operating company | 25% |
| Cross-sell potential (both directions) | 20% |
| Revenue quality (recurring/retainer share) | 15% |
| Size fit (~$1M–$20M EBITDA, buyable below GES's ~5.9x platform multiple) | 10% |
| Margin vs. GES core (~5–7%) | 10% |
| Sale readiness / actionability | 10% |
| Risk (antitrust, union, concentration), **inverted**, so 5 = lowest risk | 10% |

- **Portfolio rule:** Three candidates outscored our #8–#10 on raw score: Smart City Networks (3.65), Orchid.Events (3.60) and Event Citadel (3.60). We moved them to the watch list so the top 10 covers all seven categories rather than stacking a fourth event-tech/housing name and a second venue-services name. The gap is 0.10–0.25 points, which is inside the error of our estimates. **[Assumption]** Diversity is worth that small score trade-off at the sourcing stage.
