# GES (Global Experience Specialists): Company Research and M&A Brief

*Prepared for Dr. Lee · Research date: November 19, 2026 · Website: [ges.com](https://www.ges.com)*

> **Purpose:** Background research on GES ahead of a possible engagement to build a mergers and acquisitions (M&A) application that helps GES find, evaluate, and integrate acquisition targets. Sources are listed at the end. Where sources disagree, or where something is my own inference, I say so.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

- **What GES does:** GES is one of the two largest **general service contractors** in the trade show and exhibition industry. Freeman is the other. GES also offers experiential marketing (Spiro), event housing (onPeak), event registration and data (Visit), electrical and lighting in Canada (SHOWTECH), and, as of this month, association marketing (CSG Creative).
- **Who owns it:** Since December 31, 2024, GES has been owned by **Truelink Capital**, a Los Angeles private equity (PE) firm. Truelink bought it from Viad Corp (NYSE: VVI) for **$535M**, roughly **5.9x** 2024 adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization). Before that, GES had been inside a public conglomerate for about 55 years.
- **GES is buying companies, and the pace is picking up.** It has closed two add-on acquisitions under Truelink:
  1. **2Heads** (London experiential agency): announced December 2, 2025, folded into Spiro.
  2. **CSG Creative** (Alexandria, VA association marketing agency): announced **November 5, 2026**, run as its own operating company.
- **Buyer's stated intent:** Truelink co-founder Luke Myers said on November 5, 2026, *"we continue to see a **robust pipeline of M&A opportunities** to build on the platform."* In mid-2025 he said: *"From a consolidation standpoint, we are in the **3rd inning**… six months into our investment, the opportunity is even larger than we expected."*
- **What this means for the M&A app:** GES is a classic PE "buy-and-build" platform. It is about two years into a typical 4–7 year hold, with a long pipeline of small, fragmented targets: agencies, regional contractors, event technology, and specialty services. That is exactly the situation where deal-flow tracking, target scoring, diligence workflow, and post-merger integration tooling earn their keep.

| Metric | Figure | Source / note |
|---|---|---|

| 2024E Adjusted EBITDA (Viad guidance) | $85M–$95M | Deal coverage |
| Implied multiple | **~5.9x** EBITDA (at $90M midpoint) | PE Professional calculation |
| 2023 GES Adjusted EBITDA | $68.2M (+11.3% YoY) | Viad FY2023 results |
| 2022 GES revenue | ~$836M (GES Exhibitions $557.9M + Spiro $277.6M) | Viad FY2022 release (SEC) |
| 2022 GES Adjusted EBITDA | $61.3M (Exhibitions $34.3M, 6.1% margin; Spiro $27.0M, 9.7% margin) | Viad FY2022 release (SEC) |

> ⚠️ **Data caveat:** One trade article (PE Professional) gives 2024 GES revenue as "$258M–$278M." That figure doesn't fit Viad's reported GES revenue of roughly $830M+ in 2022, and it looks like an error. Treat GES as a **business with roughly $0.8–1.0B in revenue and EBITDA margins in the high single digits to low teens.** That is my inference from public filings; current figures are private.

**Margin story for M&A:** The core contracting business earns relatively low margins (about 5–7%). Agency, creative, data, and technology businesses tend to earn more and carry recurring retainers. So each add-on that shifts the mix toward higher-margin services makes the whole company worth more at exit. Both Truelink add-ons so far (2Heads and CSG) fit this pattern.

## 7. Acquisition Strategy: What GES and Truelink Are Signaling

### 7.1 Truelink's investment thesis (stated)
- **Barriers to entry:** "a very difficult and expensive space to enter without capital and expertise… we view the events space as highly durable, with favorable tailwinds." (Myers, 2025)
- **Consolidation:** "We are in the 3rd inning… we saw M&A as a clear driver of value and growth." (Myers, 2025)
- **Agency expansion:** GES's "strategic opportunity to further expand into the agency vertical through Spiro" was a core reason for the deal. (Myers, 2024)
- **Data and AI:** "turning each event into a scalable data collection environment, with data from one event informing a better experience across our entire event portfolio… package the insights into key takeaways for our brand partners." (Myers, 2025)
- **End-to-end:** the goal is to make GES "the premier end-to-end partner for live experiences worldwide."
- **Truelink's profile:** a middle-market PE firm with over $4.3B of assets under management. Its first fund (Truelink Capital I, 2024) raised $875M against a $600M target. It targets companies with $20M–$75M of EBITDA in business services and industrials, and it explicitly pursues "transformative add-on acquisitions."

### 7.2 Patterns in the two add-ons so far

| | 2Heads (Dec 2025) | CSG Creative (Nov 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Experiential/creative agency | Association marketing & creative agency |
| HQ | London (+ LA, Montreal) | Alexandria, Virginia |
| Seller | Founder/management-owned (40+ yrs) | Corporate carve-out from National Trade Productions (NTP) |
| Integration model | **Folded into Spiro** | **Standalone brand** as a GES operating company |
| Strategic logic | UK/EU creative footprint; strategic and digital depth | Cross-sell: attendee marketing → show execution; recurring retainers |
| Management | Retained | Founder and team retained |
| Price | Undisclosed | Undisclosed |

**What GES evidently looks for:**
1. **Capability adds, not just more scale.** Both deals add services GES didn't have, especially creative and marketing.
2. **Revenue synergies through cross-selling.** The CSG release calls out "two-way cross-sell" explicitly.
3. **Recurring revenue** (retainers) to smooth out lumpy show-cycle revenue.
4. **Keeping management and brand.** Founders stay, and brands survive where clients care about them.
5. **Geographic fill-in.** 2Heads covers the UK/Europe; CSG covers the DC-area association market.
6. **Flexible deal structures:** both founder-owned companies and corporate carve-outs.

### 7.3 Likely target categories (my analysis)

Based on GES's service gaps, Truelink's stated thesis, and what Freeman has assembled:

| Category | Rationale | Example kinds of targets |
|---|---|---|
| **Experiential / brand agencies** | Spiro build-out; higher margins | Boutique agencies in key metros or EMEA |
| **Association & event marketing** | Extends the CSG model; recurring retainers | Attendee acquisition, membership marketing, sponsorship sales firms |
| **Event technology & data** | The data/AI thesis; strengthens Visit and onPeak | Registration, lead retrieval, matchmaking/networking, event analytics, AI tools |
| **Audio-visual & production** | Freeman owns Alford Media; GES has a gap here | Regional AV and staging companies |
| **Regional general contractors** | Consolidation of a fragmented market; venue and show wins | Independent GSCs and decorators in secondary U.S. markets, Canada, Middle East, India |
| **Exhibit houses / rental & modular** | Fits Quade's push on rentals and reusable structures | Mid-size exhibit builders, rental-inventory owners (the industry has 7,000+ U.S. exhibit companies) |
| **Venue services** | GES now names "live event venue services" as a pillar | In-house venue electrical, rigging, and utility providers |
| **Sustainability services** | Carpet recycling and reusable materials are current GES themes | Event waste and reuse specialists |

## 8. Competitive Landscape

| Competitor | Profile | M&A relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **Freeman** (The Freeman Company) | The industry's largest player. In Feb 2026 it relaunched as "The Freeman Company," a **house of 9 brands** (e.g., Sparks, 3D Exhibits, Alford Media AV) | The benchmark: GES appears to be copying the house-of-brands model. Freeman is a long-time acquirer (Champion, George Fern, and Immersa in 2011, among others) |
| **Shepard Exposition Services** | Employee-owned GSC, 120 years old | Large independent; unlikely seller, possible partner |
| **Fern Expo & Event Services** | GSC; bought back by employees from Freeman, then acquired by **MSouth Equity Partners** (PE) | Another PE-backed consolidator competing for the same targets |
| **Alliance Exposition, Hargrove, regional GSCs** | Mid-size and regional contractors | Potential targets or competitors in roll-ups |
| **Large exhibit houses:** Derse (family-owned), MC² (owned by MCH Group), Skyline (rolling up its own dealers) | Compete with Spiro for custom exhibit work | Skyline's dealer buybacks show consolidation is active across the industry |
| **Mid-size exhibit houses:** Catalyst, Condit, MSM (ESOP), Steelhead, Triple20 | Specialized, often founder- or employee-owned | The universe of tuck-in candidates |

**Industry context:**
- The trade show industry is **highly fragmented**, with more than 7,000 U.S. exhibit and experiential companies. Private equity activity is "significant" (Triple20, 2026).
- Exhibitors face rising costs, which is driving demand for **rentals, modular builds, and sustainability**. GES is investing here (IMEX America 2026).
- **AI** is coming into hotel booking (onPeak) and event intelligence (Visit).

## 9. Implications for an M&A Application for GES

GES is a PE-backed platform working a "robust pipeline" in a fragmented industry. Here is what an M&A application could do for them:

### 9.1 Core modules
1. **Target universe & market map:** a searchable database of exhibit houses, agencies, GSCs, AV firms, and event-tech companies, tagged by geography, service line, size, ownership type (founder/ESOP/PE/corporate), and fit with GES operating companies (Spiro, Exhibitions, onPeak, Visit, SHOWTECH, CSG).
2. **Deal pipeline / CRM:** stages from Sourced → Contacted → NDA → IOI (indication of interest) → LOI (letter of intent) → Diligence → Close → Integrating, with owner, next step, and Truelink/GES approval gates.
3. **Target scoring:** weighted scorecards covering strategic fit, cross-sell potential, recurring-revenue share, margin uplift, geographic fill, management retention risk, and customer overlap with GES's show and organizer book.
4. **Valuation & accretion calculator:** entry multiple vs. GES's ~5.9x platform multiple (multiple arbitrage), pro-forma EBITDA, synergy modeling, earn-out and seller-note structures (Truelink itself used a seller note).
5. **Diligence workspace:** checklists, a document request list (DRL), red-flag tracking. Luke Myers' #1 deal-killer is *"when a target misses performance while in market,"* so the app should track the target's monthly performance during the process.
6. **Integration (PMI) playbooks:** two templates matching GES's two models: *fold-in* (2Heads → Spiro) and *standalone operating company* (CSG). Each covers 100-day plans, cross-sell tracking, and talent retention.
7. **Cross-sell tracker:** measures the revenue synergies GES publicly promised, e.g., CSG clients buying GES exhibition services and vice versa.
8. **Board / LP reporting:** roll-up dashboards for Truelink on deals closed, capital deployed, and synergy realization.

### 9.2 AI angles (fit with Truelink's data/AI thesis)
- Auto-source targets from trade publications (TSNN, Exhibitor, EXHIBITORLIVE lists, BizBash), association member directories (ESCA, EDPA, IAEE), and show exhibitor or contractor lists.
- Summarize target websites and news, and flag ownership changes or founder-succession signals.
- Draft outreach and NDA/IOI documents, and summarize data rooms.

### 9.3 Open questions to ask GES / Truelink
1. Who owns corporate development at GES: an in-house corp dev team, Truelink's deal team, or both?
2. What tools do they use today (spreadsheets, DealCloud, Affinity, Salesforce)?
3. Which target categories and geographies are the priority for 2027?
4. Typical target size (revenue and EBITDA band) and check size?
5. Integration preference: fold-in vs. standalone, and who decides?
6. Data security requirements (e.g., SSO, hosting inside GES/Truelink IT)?
